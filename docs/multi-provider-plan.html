Connecting more providers

Right now our app only works properly if you are with Octopus and own a Sunsynk inverter. Everyone else hits a dead end. Here is how we fix that.

The problem

We know many providers. We can only use two.

Our database already has 21 energy suppliers and 12 solar brands in it. We use them for prices and for guessing equipment. But a customer can only connect their account to two of them.

What it feels like

Meet a normal customer

She is with EDF and has a GivEnergy battery. Both are in our database. This is her sign-up today.

Step 1 She types her address Works
Step 2 She answers when her home was built Works
Step 3 “Connect Sunsynk” — but she has GivEnergy Can only skip
Step 4 “Connect Octopus” — but she is with EDF Can only skip
Result An empty dashboard. We never even asked who she is with. Nothing

This is the real problem. Not that we cannot read her GivEnergy battery — that is hard and takes time. The problem is we never asked. We already know EDF's prices. We could have estimated her bill. Instead she got a blank screen.

The idea

Three levels instead of on-or-off

Today a provider is either connected or invisible. That is why she got nothing. If we split it into three levels, everybody gets something.

1

We know them

The provider is in our list. Nothing to do with this customer yet.

She sees: EDF in the dropdown.

2

She tells us

She picks EDF and GivEnergy from a list. No password, no account. Just her word.

She gets: estimated bills using EDF's real prices, and solar figures sized to her battery. We label them “estimated”, honestly.

3

She connects

She logs in to her provider. Real data starts flowing.

She gets: real meter readings and live power. Estimates become measurements.

Level 3 is what we have today, for two providers. Level 2 is the big win — it works for all 33 providers immediately, and it needs no deal with anyone.

The work

Four jobs, in this order

Let people tell us who they are with

Show all 21 suppliers and 12 solar brands. Save the answer. Use their prices for estimates. This alone fixes the empty dashboard for most customers.

Tidy up our own code first

Today Octopus and Sunsynk are written twice, separately. We replace that with one list. Nothing changes on screen — but after this, adding a provider is a small job instead of a big one.

Do the same on the server

The server has the right design already, but the provider names are typed in by hand in three places. Make that list flexible.

Add a real third provider

Pick one and connect it properly, end to end. If jobs 1–3 were done right, this is quick — and it proves the rest works.

Job 1 is the one customers feel. Jobs 2 and 3 are invisible but make everything after them cheap. Job 4 is the test.

Technical detail — for whoever builds it

The server is already built the right way. integration-status.ts is a neutral registry: each provider registers itself at startup, and one endpoint reads them all back without knowing any provider by name. Its own comment says "octopus" | "sunsynk" | future.

Three places break that and all are small:

1. KNOWN_INTEGRATIONS = ["octopus", "sunsynk"] — a hardcoded list sitting directly above the registry designed to avoid it.
2. An if (kind === "octopus") … else if (kind === "sunsynk") chain decides who gets prompted to connect.
3. Connect routes are per-vendor (/api/sunsynk/connect). That is fine to keep — each login really is different — but the website needs one lookup table instead of two hand-written modals.

Website side: connection-state.ts declares two separate status types and there are four modals, two per vendor. These become one provider definition — id, name, logo, which fields its form needs — rendered by one generic modal.
